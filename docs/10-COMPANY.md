# The company behind the app

Researched on 2026-10-08, when the owner asked whether to launch FamStory
under an SIA or some other way, now that Premium is paid for. **This is not
tax or legal advice.** Confirm it in one conversation with an accountant
before founding anything; the numbers are the 2026 rates as published.

## 1. The decision

**A small-capital SIA (mazkapitāla SIA)**, founded online. It was already the
plan in `09-APP-STORES.md` ("the store accounts belong to a company"). The
research says why it fits this app in particular:

| Reason | What it means for FamStory |
|---|---|
| **Limited liability** | The app holds families' photographs and children's profiles. A data breach or a claim is the company's, not the founder's own property. |
| **The address on the store page** | The EU's Digital Services Act makes every trader that earns money publish an address, a phone number and an email on the App Store page (Apple asks for it since February 2025). For a person that is their home address or a P.O. box; for a company it is the company's. |
| **Google Play without the 12 testers** | New personal Play accounts must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days before they may publish. Organisation accounts do not have that rule. |
| **Tax while it grows** | An SIA pays no corporate income tax on profit it keeps, so the first year's money can go into marketing untaxed. Profit paid out to the owners is taxed at 20%. |

**Against it:** an accountant (roughly EUR 30-60 a month for a company this
size), the D-U-N-S number to wait for (§3), and annual reports.

## 2. The alternatives, and why not

- **Self-employed (saimnieciskās darbības veicējs).** Cheaper and quicker to
  start, but liability is unlimited, the address on the store page is the
  founder's, and Google's 12-testers rule applies. Tax: 25.5% personal income
  tax on profit plus 31.07% social insurance on a base of at least the
  minimum wage (EUR 780 in 2026), which is at least ~EUR 242 a month once the
  income reaches it.
- **Microenterprise tax (MUN).** 25% of turnover, not of profit. A company
  that spends most of what it earns on advertising would pay tax on money it
  never kept. A poor fit for an app.
- **A student learning company (skolēnu mācību uzņēmums, Junior Achievement
  Latvia).** No legal status of its own. Receipts up to EUR 3,000 a year are
  free of personal income tax; above that it has to register with the State
  Revenue Service within five working days. It **cannot sign Apple's or
  Google's agreements**, so it does not replace a company. What it is good
  for: competitions, fairs (around 1,900 learning companies in 2026) and a
  story the press likes.

## 3. The founder's age

Apple's paid developer membership needs the legal age of majority (18 in
Latvia), and so does Google Play Console. If the founder is younger:

- the developer accounts are opened in the company's name by an adult who
  may sign for it - a parent on the board;
- in a small-capital SIA every board member must also be a shareholder, so
  the parent is both;
- whether a minor can be a shareholder or a board member is a question for
  the Register of Enterprises (Uzņēmumu reģistrs) or a lawyer, before filing.

## 4. The numbers that matter

| | 2026 |
|---|---|
| State fee, small-capital SIA (share capital EUR 1-2,799) | EUR 20 |
| State fee, SIA with share capital from EUR 2,800 | EUR 75 |
| Small-capital SIA: founders | natural persons only, at most 5 |
| Small-capital SIA: reserve | 25% of net profit each year, until equity reaches EUR 2,800 |
| Registration, online with eParaksts | free signature, 1-3 working days |
| Corporate income tax | 0% on retained profit, 20% on distributed profit |
| Personal income tax | 25.5% up to EUR 105,300 a year, 33% above; +3% above EUR 200,000 |
| Minimum wage / non-taxable minimum | EUR 780 / EUR 550 a month |
| Social insurance, employee | 23.59% employer + 10.5% employee |
| VAT registration threshold | EUR 50,000 turnover in a calendar year |
| Store commission | 15% (Apple Small Business Program; Google on subscriptions) |

**Two things to know before they cost money:**

1. **VAT before the first advertisement.** Buying services from abroad -
   Meta or Google ads, most online tools - makes a Latvian company register
   for VAT even below the EUR 50,000 threshold (reverse charge). Since
   1 July 2025 there is a simplified registration for exactly this case
   (VAT Law s. 139²). The accountant does it; it has to happen before the
   first ad is paid for.
2. **What a subscription leaves.** Apple and Google sell Premium to the
   family (they are the merchant of record and pay the VAT on the sale), keep
   15%, and pay the company the rest. Of EUR 4.50 a month, roughly EUR 3.15
   reaches the company.

## 5. The order

1. **Age and roles** (§3), and one conversation with an accountant about
   this page.
2. **Found the SIA** online at the Register of Enterprises, with eParaksts.
3. **A bank account** for the company.
4. **D-U-N-S** for the company (free): through Apple's D-U-N-S lookup in
   about five working days, or from Dun & Bradstreet in up to 30.
5. **The domain** famstory.lv (free on 2026-09-27) and the support mailbox.
6. Then everything in `12-LAUNCH-RUNBOOK.md`, which starts from a company
   that exists.
