# Thirty short videos

For TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, in Latvian, 15-40 seconds
each (docs/11-MARKETING.md §2.3). Each has its first line - the hook that has
to hold somebody in the first two seconds - what is on screen, and the last
card.

**Four formats**, because the research says to test a few formats with many
variations and keep what holds (docs/11-MARKETING.md §1):

- **A. A memory that stays** - one feeling, one feature.
- **B. The family's chaos, sorted** - a problem everybody recognises.
- **C. A pupil builds an app** - the founder's story, in public.
- **D. The days of the year** - for the dates the app already knows.

**Rules for every video** (docs/11-MARKETING.md §4): no child's face without
the parents' clear yes - hands, objects, a grandparent's voice and the app's
screens tell the story; real families only with their permission; the end
card says what the app is, never a price.

The last card is the same for all: **FamStory - ģimenes hronika. App Store un
Google Play.**

## How to read the results

Post each hook once, then compare after 48 hours:

- **Watched to the end** (%) - whether the video holds.
- **Shares and saves** - whether people want it for their own family.
- **Profile visits** - whether they want the app.

Views alone mean little on a new account. The format with the best
watch-through and shares gets the next five variations; the weakest is
dropped. `test/hooklab` (the hook study) and `C:\dev\influencer` (small
family accounts to work with) help with both halves.
